## Verdict

At [$225.51](#ref=quote.price), NVIDIA looks **fairly valued**. My base case is worth [$282.96](#ref=dcf[g=0.3,r=0.115,tg=0.03,model=fcf,base=127006000000,years=5,fade=5].perShare), but the range is extremely wide, from [$82.10](#ref=dcf[g=0.1,r=0.125,tg=0.025,model=fcf,base=127006000000,years=5,fade=5].perShare) in a digestion bear case to [$541.97](#ref=dcf[g=0.4,r=0.11,tg=0.035,model=fcf,base=127006000000,years=5,fade=5].perShare) in the bull case. The business is exceptional: fiscal 2026 operating margin was [60.4%](#ref=ratio.FY2026.operatingMargin) and ROIC was [145.7%](#ref=ratio.FY2026.roic). The new development is on the cash side, though. NVIDIA is now extending credit to its largest customers, and operating cash flow fell well short of earnings in the latest quarter.

## The debate

**What the market assumes.** The stock trades at [28.91](#ref=quote.trailingPE) times trailing earnings but only [14.38](#ref=quote.forwardPE) times forward earnings. That means the price already assumes consensus is right: revenue of [$412B](#ref=est.0y.revenueAvg) this fiscal year (up [90.6%](#ref=est.0y.revenueGrowth)), then [$683B](#ref=est.+1y.revenueAvg) next year, with EPS rising from [$9.31](#ref=est.0y.epsAvg) to [$15.68](#ref=est.+1y.epsAvg).

**The discount rate matters a lot.** The default model uses a [14.1%](#ref=val.inputs.discountRate) discount rate because NVIDIA's 3-year beta is [2.1](#ref=risk.beta). At that rate the price implies [32.9%](#ref=val.impliedGrowth) annual free-cash-flow growth for five years. I think a beta of 2 over-penalises a net-cash company, so I use [11.5%](#ref=dcf[g=0.3,r=0.115,tg=0.03,model=fcf,base=127006000000,years=5,fade=5].discountRate). At that rate the price implies [25.7%](#ref=dcf[g=0.3,r=0.115,tg=0.03,model=fcf,base=127006000000,years=5,fade=5].impliedGrowth) growth. Consensus revenue growth for the next two years is far above that, so the near-term bar looks achievable.

**The real question is years 3–5.** In the base case, [60.2%](#ref=dcf[g=0.3,r=0.115,tg=0.03,model=fcf,base=127006000000,years=5,fade=5].terminalShare) of value comes from the terminal value. The valuation is therefore a bet on AI infrastructure spending staying high after the current build-out, which no dataset can settle.

## Business & moat

NVIDIA sells accelerated-computing platforms: GPUs, NVLink and InfiniBand/Ethernet networking, systems and software. Its customers are hyperscalers, AI labs and "neoclouds". The moat is wide and rests on three things:
- **Software and ecosystem lock-in.** CUDA and the libraries around it.
- **System-level integration.** NVIDIA sells whole systems, not only chips.
- **Pace.** The company says it is ["bringing new advanced architectures on a one-year product cadence"](#ref=filing.risk).

The numbers support this:
- **Gross margin:** [71.1%](#ref=ratio.FY2026.grossMargin) on [$216B](#ref=fin.FY2026.revenue) of revenue.
- **Peer margins:** trailing operating margin was [66.2%](#ref=quote.ttm.operatingMargin), against [54.3%](#ref=peer[AVGO].operatingMargin) at Broadcom, the strongest custom-silicon competitor.

The cost of that dominance is concentration. In the 10-K, ["sales to one direct customer represented 22% of total revenue and sales to another direct customer represented 14% of total revenue"](#ref=filing.risk).

## Financial quality

Growth is real and still speeding up:
- **Quarterly revenue:** [$81.6B](#ref=fin.Q2026-04.revenue) in the April quarter, then [$96.2B](#ref=fin.Q2026-07.revenue) in the July quarter.
- **Fiscal 2026:** revenue grew [65.5%](#ref=ratio.FY2026.revenueGrowth) and free cash flow was [$96.7B](#ref=fin.FY2026.fcf), a [44.8%](#ref=ratio.FY2026.fcfMargin) margin.
- **Balance sheet:** net cash, and a small reduction in share count ([−1.2%](#ref=ratio.FY2026.shareCountChange)).

Cash conversion is getting worse:
- **Annual trend:** operating cash flow was [1.29×](#ref=ratio.FY2023.cfoToNetIncome) net income in fiscal 2023, but only [0.86×](#ref=ratio.FY2026.cfoToNetIncome) in fiscal 2026.
- **Latest quarter:** operating cash flow of [$24.1B](#ref=fin.Q2026-07.cfo) against net income of [$59.7B](#ref=fin.Q2026-07.netIncome), after [$50.3B](#ref=fin.Q2026-04.cfo) in the April quarter.
- **Non-operating gains:** in the April quarter, net income of [$58.3B](#ref=fin.Q2026-04.netIncome) exceeded operating income of [$53.5B](#ref=fin.Q2026-04.operatingIncome). Part of recent earnings therefore comes from outside the core business, most likely gains on investments. I have not verified that yet (see open questions).

## Forensic read

The engine flags [3](#ref=flags.count) issues, and the Beneish M-score of [-1.15](#ref=score.beneish) sits above the manipulation threshold. Some of this is mechanical: the Beneish model penalises fast sales growth, and NVIDIA's growth is exceptional. The receivables increase is the flag that matters, and there is a documented explanation:

- **Receivables jumped.** They rose from [$40.7B](#ref=fin.Q2026-04.receivables) to [$63.1B](#ref=fin.Q2026-07.receivables) in one quarter, while revenue grew far less in proportion.
- **The 10-Q explains why.** For investment-grade customers NVIDIA may provide ["longer payment terms ranging from 90 days up to one year to assist customers with large data center builds"](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm).
- **Five customers hold most of it.** Five direct customers account for [22%, 14%, 13%, 11% and 10% of the receivables balance](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm).
- **Inventory doubled in a year:** from [$15.0B](#ref=fin.Q2025-07.inventory) to [$31.6B](#ref=fin.Q2026-07.inventory).
- **Supply commitments are large.** They stand at [$279B](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm).

**Reading:** this is disclosed customer financing, not accounting manipulation. It still changes the risk profile. NVIDIA now carries credit risk on a handful of AI builders, and it has committed to supply sized for continued growth. If demand pauses, the unwind happens through receivables, inventory write-downs and purchase obligations at the same time. The Piotroski score of [4](#ref=score.piotroski) reflects the same deterioration in accruals and liquidity trends.

## Bull case / Bear case

**Bull**
- Consensus revenue of [$683B](#ref=est.+1y.revenueAvg) next year proves conservative, as recent beats suggest. Growth then stays above the base-case rate.
- The one-year product cadence keeps gross margin above 70% even as custom ASICs grow.
- The receivables are investment-grade and short-term, so cash conversion normalises once deliveries level off.

**Bear**
- Customer financing pulls demand forward. When hyperscaler capex growth slows, receivables, inventory and supply commitments unwind together.
- Custom silicon (Broadcom-designed ASICs) takes a growing share of inference and caps pricing.
- With two direct customers making up over a third of revenue (see concentration above), one customer's capex decision can move a whole year.

## Key risks

1. **AI capex cycle turns.** The valuation leans on a terminal value that assumes spending stays high.
2. **Credit and concentration.** A few customers owe most of the receivables, on terms of up to a year.
3. **Export controls.** China licensing rules have already changed product mix; further rules could remove revenue with little notice.
4. **Competition.** Custom ASICs and AMD pressure pricing at the margin.
5. **Volatility.** Volatility is [37.8%](#ref=risk.vol1y) and the stock can fall hard even if the business stays intact. The strong [75.8%](#ref=risk.ret.3Y) annualised 3-year return (vs [21.3%](#ref=risk.bench.3Y) for the S&P 500) raises expectations.

## What to monitor

1. **Cash conversion:** operating cash flow / net income back above 0.85×. The first test is the next quarter.
2. **Receivable days:** staying below 75 (fiscal 2026 was [65 d](#ref=ratio.FY2026.receivableDays)).
3. **Gross margin:** holding above 70%.
4. **Revenue growth:** staying above what the price needs.
5. **Growth implied by the price** (reverse DCF): staying below 35%.

## Open questions

- How large were non-operating gains (investment mark-ups) in the last two quarters, and are they recurring?
- What share of revenue now ships on extended terms, and is that share still growing?
- How are the supply commitments split between near-term and multi-year obligations?
